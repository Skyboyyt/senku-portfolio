Web3 platform / Full-stack TypeScript

Blacklist

A Web3 anti-scam and project-verification platform I’m building.

Blacklist helps people research crypto projects, wallets, tokens and contracts before they trust them, and gives victims and researchers an evidence-based, accountable way to report, review and contest suspicious activity.

Running on a public test environment with real blockchain data. It is not launched yet: public risk ratings stay off until they are calibrated, and image uploads accept test images only. The screenshots show an earlier demo build.

Behind the project : Blacklist
My role
Building the platform end to end
Status
Live test build, not launched
Stack
Next.js / TypeScript / Fastify / PostgreSQL
Testing
Hundreds of automated tests & Playwright
  • Wallet & transaction research Look up Ethereum and Solana wallets, tokens and transactions, with readable balances, token holdings and transfer history from live blockchain providers.
  • Reports with image evidence Members report scams with screenshots. Every image is checked and re-encoded by an isolated processor that strips hidden data, so staff only ever see a clean copy.
  • Careful moderation Staff sign in with security keys, severe findings need a second reviewer, and the projects involved can respond and appeal. Every decision is recorded.
  • Fairness built in Being linked to a wallet does not mean being guilty. Findings stay sourced and open to challenge, risk is shown in bands instead of a score, and a low band never means “safe”.

The problem

Crypto scams spread faster than the facts. Blacklist aims to give people one careful place to check a project or wallet before they commit.

How I’m building it

In planned phases, each reviewed before the next: the frontend on sample data first, then the backend foundation and accounts, blockchain research, reports and moderation, the risk engine and admin tools, and image evidence.

What’s next

Launch readiness: an independent security review, a legal review, recovery drills and a production setup, before anyone relies on it for real.

Game development / Unity WebGL

Battle of Shinkai

An AxoRonin samurai battle game you can play right in the browser.

A 3D sword-combat game I’m building in Unity 6 and shipping as a WebGL test build for phone and desktop browsers. Pick a fighter, chain skills together, and fight your way across the continent of Shinkai.

Play Battle of Shinkai (opens the game) View the build on GitHub (opens in a new tab)
Behind the project : Battle of Shinkai
My role
Game development
Status
Playable test build
Engine
Unity 6 / WebGL
Platforms
Desktop & mobile browsers
  • Sword combat Skills like Dash Slash, Shadow Assault, Dragon Strike, Void Slash and Spinning Slash, plus a Saiyen Mode power-up and Perfect Parries.
  • Adventure map Villages across the Shinkai world map, five missions each, with Easy, Medium and Hard difficulty.
  • Private 1v1 multiplayer Create a room, share its code, and duel a friend. The room server decides hits, damage and the result.
  • AxoRonin NFT fighters An optional, read-only Solana wallet link lets holders play as their AxoRonin. Free characters are always available, and the game never asks for a signature or transaction.

The direction

A samurai battle game set in the AxoRonin world, bringing my interest in game development and Web3 together in a project of my own.

How it has grown

Built in phases: a combat overhaul with fluid whole-body animation and hit-stop, new fighting skills and Saiyen Mode, then the Adventure world map and private 1v1 rooms.

Play safely

Guests can play without signing in. Connecting a wallet is optional and read-only; it is only used to check which AxoRonin characters you own.
