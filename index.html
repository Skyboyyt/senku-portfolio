Game development / Unity WebGL

Battle of Shinkai

An AxoRonin samurai battle game you can play right in the browser.

A 3D sword-combat game I’m building in Unity 6 and shipping as a WebGL test build for phone and desktop browsers. Pick a fighter, chain skills together, and fight your way across the continent of Shinkai.

  • Sword combatSkills like Dash Slash, Shadow Assault, Dragon Strike, Void Slash and Spinning Slash, plus a Saiyen Mode power-up and Perfect Parries.
  • Adventure mapVillages across the Shinkai world map, five missions each, with Easy, Medium and Hard difficulty.
  • Private 1v1 multiplayerCreate a room, share its code, and duel a friend. The room server decides hits, damage and the result.
  • AxoRonin NFT fightersAn optional, read-only Solana wallet link lets holders play as their AxoRonin. Free characters are always available, and the game never asks for a signature or transaction.

Web3 platform / Next.js & TypeScript

Blacklist

A Web3 anti-scam and project-verification platform I’m building.

Blacklist helps people research crypto projects, wallets, tokens and contracts before they trust them, and gives victims and researchers an evidence-based, accountable way to report, review and contest suspicious activity.

  • Research & project profilesA searchable project directory and profile pages that keep blockchain facts, community reports, moderator-verified findings and outside sources clearly apart.
  • Reports with reviewMembers submit reports; staff review them, with second approvals for severe findings, a revision history, responses from the projects involved, and appeals.
  • Fairness built inThe rule is that being linked to a wallet does not mean being guilty, so every finding stays sourced, reviewable and open to challenge.
  • Security & accessibilityAccount security screens, a written threat model, and automated accessibility checks run in light and dark themes and on phone-sized screens.

Behind the project: Blacklist

The problem

Crypto scams spread faster than the facts. Blacklist aims to give people one careful place to check a project or wallet before they commit.

How I’m building it

Frontend first, in planned phases: the design system and app shell, research previews, accounts and security, project pages, staff review, and member submissions are built, followed by a quality and accessibility pass.

What’s next

The backend: a database, an API and background jobs, with security checks that have to pass before any real data goes in.
